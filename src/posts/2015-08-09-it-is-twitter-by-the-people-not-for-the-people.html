Social media are an increasing part of our daily life. They made conversations asynchronously and diminished geographic limitations (boyd et al., 2010). As one of the most popular social media Twitter plays an important role in this (Duggan et al., 2015). The Twitter platform is a centrally created product, but the product development was inverse. When Twitter started with Jack Dorsey’s tweet on 21 March 2006 there were no mentions, hashtags and retweets (Feinberg, 2013). Just simple status updates in 140 characters: a web-based microblogging service. The new options for user collaboration via user-to-user exchange made it a social software phenomenon that grew exponentially (Honeycutt & Herring, 2009). This analysis of Twitter will be performed as a literature review with the research question:

How did the users of Twitter independently develop features that define the platform?

The literature is gathered via Google Scholar with the search queries: hashtag, inverse, mention, retweet and Twitter. For each combination of queries the first 20 results were considered. Next to the academic sources also articles by the mainstream media are included. Twitter is a young product that is constantly in development. To cover all developments extensively it is not possible to only rely on academic sources. This essay is structured along the most well known features of Twitter. Chronically sorted based on their date of informal introduction. First the creation of the mention is discussed, second the retweet, third the hashtag and fourth the other user incentives. This essay ends with a brief conclusion.

Mention

While the founders were developing their product the users started experimenting. Robert Anderson first addressed a tweet with a mention on 3 November 2006 to Buzz Anderson: “@ buzz - you broke your thumb and youre still twittering? that's some serious devotion” (Twitter, 2006). The at-sign is not new on the internet. It is well known for for being part of an e-mail address. Ray Tomlin choose it when he sent the first network e-mail in 1971 (Hicks, 2012; Metz, 2012). It was preferable because this sign did not appear in names and had no significance in any integrated development environment he was using: no risk of ambiguity (Tomlin, 2015). This decision is also vital for Twitter. Before the e-mail the at-sign was used to express the rate or cost per unit. Research shows that Florentine merchant Francesco Lapi already used it in 1536 (Long, 2009). Due to the use of different alphabets in countries the at-sign was not present at each keyboard (Allman, 2012). The rise of the internet changed this. Thus Anderson was also wise that he chose a symbol that is now on almost all keyboards. This idea radically changed the medium from a one-way to a two-way-communication tool (Seward, 2013). Twenty days later developer Neil Crosby slightly changed the convention by removing the space between @ and buzz (Twitter, 2006). In May 2007 this function got implemented by Twitter with hyperlinks and a dedicated page Replies.

Retweet

This success of this new feature further stimulated the experiments. In April 2007 Eric Rice shared for the first time a tweet preceded by ReTweet. He used the text of Jesse Malthus: “ReTweet: jmalthus @spin Yes! Web2.0 is about social media, and guess what people like to be social about? Themselves. Social Narcissism” (Twitter, 2007). The first retweet with just the abbreviation RT was a year later. When TDavid copied the tweet from BreakingNewsOn by adding RT (Twitter, 2008). At first people did not saw the need for the use of retweets (Seward, 2013). Although there are multiple reasons to retweet. The most common is to share a tweet to a new audience. Other goals are to comment on a tweet, publicly agree with a user, loyalty or to archive tweets. In November 2009 Twitter introduced the feature to retweet without manually adding RT. This made it easier and more friendly to retweet (boyd et al., 2010). The RT-prefix and username were no longer included in the 140 characters limit. This saved users from creative solutions to shorten original tweets. Which sometimes would affect the intention of the tweet and cause misinterpretation.

Hashtag

The most well known feature of Twitter came in the summer of 2007. Chris Messina was discussing online with other users the options and constraints for groups within Twitter (Get Satisfaction, 2007). On 23 August 2007 Messina tweeted: “how do you feel about using # (pound) for groups. As in #barcamp [msg]?” (Twitter, 2007). Which makes him the inventor of the hashtag (Edwards, 2013). However it was Stowe Boyd (2007) who dubbed it ‘hashtag’. For unknown reasons Twitter does not credit Messina for the creation of the hashtag. It just says that it was created organically by their users (Twitter, 2014). The usage of the pound sign to categorize has indeed a longer history than Twitter. Actually it started on Internet Relay Chat (IRC). Here the pound sign was used to name channels. According to Messina the use of the hashtag on IRC showed that users prefer categorisation. And it was also used within the micro blogging service Jaiku. Although there the pound sign always needed to be the first word of the message.

Messina (2014) consciously did not patent the hashtag. He expected that license restrictions would limit the adoption. While he was hoping for broad-based adoption and support. Like other developments within the internet he thinks they should not be owned by anyone. He derives satisfaction and value by seeing it used. Messina even further developed his idea to a broader subscription concept, although this feature never got adopted. That is because the power of the hashtag is its simplicity. The hashtag should improve both the search for a topic and the option to participate. The folksonomic approach as Messina (2007) described it. There are no pre-established groups with more rights for expression. In case of collision a collective socially engineered solution should resolve the problem. Most important, the hashtags can be used at the beginning, end or in the middle of a Tweet. With only 140 characters this is a crucial advantage.

Twitter initially rejected the idea. “[Twitter] told me flat out, ‘These things are for nerds. They’re never going to catch on.’” (Zak, 2013). They would rather use machine learning to tag conversations and create channels. The hashtags became mainstream after the San Diego forest fires in October 2007. It was Messina himself to promote the use of hashtags. In a direct message he asked journalist Nate Ritter to use #sandiegofire in his tweets (Zak, 2013). Like many other mobile innovations it got a major boost after the South by Southwest festival in Texas. However it took until July 2009 before it came an official feature. Together with the introduction of the trending topics. Which is not exclusive to hashtags, although logically dominated by it. In 2013 even social media leader Facebook introduced the hashtag in their feeds (Lindley, 2013). According to Ma et al. (2012) hashtags are changing the way people are accessing and disseminating information online. The hashtag is not only present within Twitter but also increasingly influencing the daily life. Research by the University of Oxford shows that children use hashtags more in regular texts (Pickles, 2015). Since June 2014 the word is also part of the Oxford English Dictionary (2014). This digital sidekick transferred from the online to the offline universe.

Other user initiatives

Sharing relevant links is one of the major activities on Twitter (Honeycutt & Herring, 2009). However a link takes a large share of the 140 character limit. Therefor users started using URL shortening services like Bitly and TinyURL. A type of service that started in the newsgroup environments. On 7 june 2011 Twitter (2011) activated their automatic URL shortening service for all its users. Optically nothing changed, although behind the scenes all links were shortened. Giving the users more characters for their accompanying message. Twitter made URL shorting a mainstream feature that also transferred to e-mails, newspapers and other media. The same process can be identified for photos. To add photos to their tweets users started using services like TwitPic and yfrog. The photo would be included in the tweet via a short link. Instead of the URL shortening services were these services especially created for Twitter users. On 1 June 2011 Twitter (2011) launched its own photo service within Twitter. This ruined the business model of the external services. Market leader Twitpic was not even approached before the launch (Siegler, 2011). Twitter choose minor player Photobucket instead to host the pictures.

Not all user inventions become a success. As follow-up on the hashtags Howard Lindzon, the owner of StockTwits, came up with the cashtags (Hentschel & Alonso, 2014). Similar to the hashtag the cashtag is a combination of a dollar-sign with a stock symbol. StockTwits is a website where users can easily follow tweets from investment professionals. It is an independent company that functions by using Twitter’s API. This is thereby a slightly different inverse approach because it was created from an external business perspective. Twitter made the cashtags an official feature in 2012 by making them clickable as hashtags (Bohn, 2012). Which influenced the business perspective of StockTwits negatively, because it decreased the need for a separate site. Nowadays the cashtag is not very popular with the mainstream users of Twitter.

Some features were not even implemented by Twitter. The slashtag, also introduced by Chris Messina (2009), is meant to simplify references as via, cc (carbon copy) and by. This used to be encapsulated by parentheses. The slash saves one character. Next to the retweet the abbreviations for hat tip or heard through (HT), modified tweet (MT) and overheard (OH) were introduced by the users. Although these are shorter, they are also not considered as widespread adopted (Charpentier, 2014).

Conclusions

Twitter is now a 25 billion company and more important holds a solid position in society. The hashtag is present in online and offline environments. While Twitter usernames accompanied with the @-sign are widely promoted like the addresses of websites. As a result the mention, retweets and especially the hashtags resulted in extensive academic and non-academic research. Unwittingly Twitter became a testing ground for efficient online interaction. Not only because the maximum of 140 characters, but also the lack of embeddable content. Resulting in creative solutions and new social rules by the users. Later adopted by Twitter into functions. For all these developments it must thankful to its users. Not only because they intensively use the product, but especially because they improve it. Although Twitter responds egoistic and disrespectful in the process of adding a new feature. A strange behavior if you consider that Twitter flourished by the creativity and incentives of its users.
