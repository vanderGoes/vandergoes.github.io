The HEINEKEN Company is innovating its 157 years old business with data and analytics. Recommendation systems increasingly support our business processes by filtering information for insights. For example, a system for our sales squad in advising bars on their beer portfolio. However, our international scale is both a benefit and a challenge. Our goal is to leverage our international presence and harmonize the recommender infrastructure across subsidiaries. With the Machine Learning Operations method, we create standardized recommender systems on the Azure platform. A culture of self-service, automation, and collaboration enables us to scale recommender systems to local subsidiaries fast. This work was accepted to the RecSys conference.

With subsidiaries in more than 70 countries, the HEINEKEN Company is the most international brewer. Within these subsidiaries, the demand for analytics solutions increases, like recommender systems that filter information to predict preferences. This supports our sales squad to better advise outlets (e.g. bars) on which beers to offer to their customers. In the current situation, these subsidiaries independently developed their recommender systems. Resulting in various technology choices and diversity in data sources. This lack of standardization is a major engineering challenge from a global perspective as it is complicated to support, expensive to maintain, and does not leverage HEINEKEN's scale. In the desired situation, local recommender systems are harmonized across subsidiaries. Ensuring a high delivery velocity without technical debt. Accordingly, this work concentrates on how to scale standardized recommender systems within an enterprise, while retaining decentralized ownership and incorporating local specifications?

MLOps approach

We draw from experience in addressing this challenge with the Machine Learning Operations (MLOps) method. MLOps consists of practices to launch systems with attention to efficiency, reliability, and reproducibility. It is the combination of ML and DevOps (Development Operations). An essential merger, as machine learning solutions are shaped by code and data. Compared to regular software engineering that only covers code. Our MLOps life cycle has four stages because we believe that data management is a fundamental part of successful recommender systems. This life cycle is not a one-way or singular process. The recommender system evolves by parallel actions.

Machine Learning Operations life cycle

The MLOps method is executed five best practices:

  • Pipeline automation
  • Data availability
  • Exchangeable artifacts
  • Observability
  • Policy-based security

Use case

Following this MLOps approach, we created a recommender system that scales within an enterprise. The recommender system is a decoupled architecture of internal solution patterns on the Azure cloud. The Data Management stage ingests the raw data from the upstream service and transforms this into a centrally available, standardized dataset. In the Modelling stage, this data is modeled with an Azure ML workspace that functions as the recommender engine. The Development stage creates the internal solution patterns that ensure both reliability and interoperability between the resources. The Operations stage uses automated deployment of resources with CI/CD pipelines. This stage also provides local stakeholders with accessible observability to discover and inspect possible issues. This standardized solution decreases the engineering time per subsidiary while keeping the agility to effectively incorporate local specifications and distribute ownership.

Recommender system

Standardizing the recommender systems across subsidiaries with resources and solution patterns simplifies the collaboration between colleagues. They can easily exchange knowledge, machine learning models, and other artifacts. Internal tutorials are aligned and where needed the global team provides additional support. Thereby benefiting from the global scale and experience of HEINEKEN.

Learnings

Self-Service, Automation & Collaboration

We addressed the challenge of scaling local recommender systems within an enterprise. From this process, we learned that engineering should promote three principles: self-service, automation, and collaboration. These principles empower subsidiaries to develop recommender systems that are harmonized throughout the company. Thereby leveraging HEINEKEN's scale to achieve a high delivery velocity while still incorporating local specifications. We believe that these engineering principles are not bound to recommender systems and are valuable to other enterprises.
